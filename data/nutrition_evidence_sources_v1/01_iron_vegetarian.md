# Dietary Iron in Vegetarian Eating Patterns

**Source ID:** `synthetic_iron_vegetarian_v1`  
**Source type:** Synthetic prototype evidence  
**Review status:** Approved for prototype evaluation  
**Clinical scope:** Nutrition assessment only

## Purpose

This source helps a nutrition professional prepare questions about iron intake when a patient follows a vegetarian eating pattern. It does not establish iron deficiency, anemia, or a treatment plan.

## Evidence statements

1. Vegetarian diets can provide iron through foods such as beans, lentils, tofu, seeds, nuts, leafy vegetables, and fortified grain products.
2. Iron from plant foods is absorbed less consistently than iron from animal foods, so both the amount eaten and the composition of the meal are relevant.
3. Including a vitamin-C-rich food in a meal can support absorption of plant-derived iron.
4. Some meal components can reduce iron absorption, but their presence alone is not a reason to eliminate otherwise nutritious foods.
5. A vegetarian eating pattern does not by itself demonstrate inadequate iron intake or iron deficiency.

## Consultation implications

A nutrition assessment may clarify usual iron-containing foods, their frequency, foods combined in the same meal, use and tolerance of prescribed supplements, and laboratory information already obtained through medical care.

Fatigue and similar symptoms are nonspecific. If iron deficiency or anemia is not known medical context, the nutrition professional may identify the need for medical assessment but should not assign a diagnosis.

## Supported uses

- Identifying missing information about dietary iron sources.
- Asking about meal combinations relevant to plant-iron absorption.
- Reviewing adherence or tolerance when a supplement was prescribed by an authorized clinician.
- Connecting nutrition assessment with existing laboratory results.

## Boundaries

- Do not infer deficiency from vegetarian status.
- Do not infer anemia from fatigue, diet, or a single nonspecific symptom.
- Do not initiate, discontinue, or change a prescribed supplement.
- Do not invent laboratory values or medical instructions.

## Applicability limits

Individual needs can vary with age, pregnancy status, blood loss, medical conditions, medications, and existing treatment. Those factors require patient-specific clarification.
