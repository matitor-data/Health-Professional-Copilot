# Endurance Fueling, Hydration, and Recovery Assessment

**Source ID:** `synthetic_endurance_v1`  
**Source type:** Synthetic prototype evidence  
**Review status:** Approved for prototype evaluation  
**Clinical scope:** General performance-nutrition assessment

## Purpose

This source supports consultation preparation for a generally healthy adult participating in endurance training. It is not a medical treatment protocol or an individualized fueling prescription.

## Evidence statements

1. The relevance of planned carbohydrate intake increases with the duration and demand of an endurance session.
2. Pre-exercise food choices should account for timing, session demands, usual intake, and gastrointestinal tolerance.
3. A fueling strategy intended for competition should first be evaluated during training.
4. Hydration requirements vary with sweat losses, environment, exercise demands, body size, clothing, equipment, and access to fluids.
5. Water can be sufficient in some shorter or less demanding settings; a sports drink is not automatically necessary for every athlete or session.
6. Drinking as much fluid as possible is not an appropriate universal strategy because excessive intake can also create risk.
7. Recovery priorities depend on the effort completed and the time available before the next demanding session.
8. Total daily energy, carbohydrate, protein, fluid intake, rest, and training schedule provide more context than a single recovery snack.

## Consultation implications

Useful questions cover sport and event, weekly training load, duration and intensity of key sessions, food before training, intake during longer sessions, fluid plan, estimated sweat loss, gastrointestinal symptoms, recovery meals, time between demanding sessions, goals, and recent changes in weight or energy intake.

Specific intake targets should not be generated until sufficient case information is available. Endurance participation alone is not evidence of underfueling, dehydration, or a need for electrolyte products.

## Supported uses

- Identifying missing information about endurance fueling and hydration.
- Considering carbohydrate availability during prolonged sessions.
- Assessing recovery intake when training demands make recovery relevant.
- Individualizing questions according to conditions and reported losses.

## Boundaries

- Do not prescribe one carbohydrate or fluid target to every athlete.
- Do not assume sports drinks or electrolyte products are necessary.
- Do not infer dehydration or low energy availability from training status alone.
- Do not treat general performance guidance as medical management.

## Applicability limits

Medical conditions, medications, pregnancy, heat illness history, disordered-eating risk, acute symptoms, and unusually extreme environments can require medical or specialist assessment beyond this source.
