# Fiber and Sweetened Drinks in Known Prediabetes

**Source ID:** `synthetic_prediabetes_fiber_v1`  
**Source type:** Synthetic prototype evidence  
**Review status:** Approved for prototype evaluation  
**Clinical scope:** Nutrition assessment only

## Purpose

This source supports preparation for a nutrition consultation when prediabetes is already documented. It focuses on fiber-containing foods and drinks with added sugar.

## Evidence statements

1. Vegetables, fruits, legumes, and whole grains are common dietary sources of fiber.
2. Fiber-rich foods can contribute to a sustainable eating pattern relevant to glucose and weight management.
3. Sweetened beverages can provide added sugar and energy without the fiber found in intact plant foods.
4. Replacing a routinely consumed sweetened drink with water or another drink containing little or no added sugar can be an option to explore.
5. Whole fruit should not automatically be treated as equivalent to a drink containing added sugar.
6. A reported food or beverage habit cannot establish prediabetes; the condition must come from known medical context.

## Consultation implications

Useful areas to clarify include the types and frequency of fiber-containing foods, typical beverages, serving sizes and frequency of sweetened drinks, meal and snack patterns, activity, relevant goals, barriers to change, and current medical follow-up.

Recommendations should reflect the patient's actual pattern. Prediabetes alone is not enough to assume high sweetened-drink intake, low fiber intake, or a need for weight loss.

## Supported uses

- Asking about usual fiber-containing foods.
- Clarifying the amount and frequency of drinks with added sugar.
- Exploring lower-added-sugar substitutions when the reported pattern makes them relevant.
- Identifying practical barriers to sustainable change.

## Boundaries

- Do not diagnose diabetes or prediabetes.
- Do not assume the patient consumes sweetened drinks.
- Do not equate all dietary sugars or carbohydrate-containing foods.
- Do not replace medical monitoring with dietary advice.

## Applicability limits

Food access, medication use, medical advice, activity, gastrointestinal tolerance, cultural preferences, and personal goals can change the relevance of these considerations.
