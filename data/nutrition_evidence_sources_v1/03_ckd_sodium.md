# Sodium Assessment in Known Chronic Kidney Disease

**Source ID:** `synthetic_ckd_sodium_v1`  
**Source type:** Synthetic prototype evidence  
**Review status:** Approved for prototype evaluation  
**Clinical scope:** Nutrition assessment only

## Purpose

This source supports sodium-related nutrition assessment when chronic kidney disease is documented in the patient's medical context.

## Evidence statements

1. Sodium intake can be relevant to fluid balance, blood pressure, and swelling in a person with chronic kidney disease.
2. Packaged meals, processed meats, salty snacks, restaurant foods, sauces, condiments, and seasoning blends can be meaningful sodium sources.
3. Food-label information can help identify where sodium enters the reported diet.
4. Sodium goals should reflect the patient's existing renal plan rather than a universal target generated from the diagnosis alone.
5. Some salt substitutes provide potassium and should not be recommended automatically when potassium management is unknown.
6. Chronic kidney disease does not imply that every patient has the same potassium, phosphorus, protein, or fluid instructions.

## Consultation implications

The consultation may clarify packaged and restaurant food frequency, salt added in cooking or at the table, sauces and condiments, label-reading habits, and instructions already given by the renal care team.

The professional should distinguish an observed high-sodium pattern from missing information. If intake is not described, the correct output is a question or information gap rather than a conclusion.

## Supported uses

- Assessing reported sodium sources.
- Clarifying existing individualized renal nutrition instructions.
- Reviewing label-reading practices.
- Flagging uncertainty before discussing potassium-containing salt substitutes.

## Boundaries

- Do not diagnose chronic kidney disease.
- Do not apply identical restrictions to all patients with kidney disease.
- Do not infer a high-sodium diet when intake is not reported.
- Do not recommend a potassium-based salt substitute without relevant medical context.

## Applicability limits

Kidney function, laboratory results, blood pressure, medications, dialysis status, symptoms, and the treating team's plan can materially change nutrition priorities.
